# The Relative Pronoun *joka* ("which / that / who")

## 🔹 Singular Forms

| Case        | Form   | Example sentence | Translation |
|-------------|--------|------------------|-------------|
| **Nominative** (subject) | **joka** | *Tämä on päätös, **joka** tehtiin eilen.* | This is the decision **which** was made yesterday. |
| **Genitive** (possessive) | **jonka** | *Tämä on päätös, **jonka** syyt ymmärrän.* | This is the decision **whose** reasons I understand. |
| **Partitive** (object, required by some verbs) | **jota** | *Tämä on päätös, **jota** kadun.* | This is the decision **which** I regret. |
| **Illative** (into which) | **johon** | *Tämä on talo, **johon** muutan.* | This is the house **into which** I'm moving. |
| **Inessive** (in which) | **jossa** | *Tämä on maa, **jossa** asun.* | This is the country **in which** I live. |
| **Elative** (from which) | **josta** | *Tämä on kirja, **josta** pidän.* | This is the book **from which** I like. |
| **Adessive** (on which) | **jolla** | *Tämä on auto, **jolla** ajan.* | This is the car **with which** I drive. |
| **Ablative** (from on which) | **jolta** | *Tämä on mies, **jolta** sain kirjan.* | This is the man **from whom** I got the book. |
| **Allative** (onto which) | **jolle** | *Tämä on ystävä, **jolle** soitin.* | This is the friend **to whom** I called. |

## ✅ Key Takeaway

- *joka* works like a chameleon: it **changes case** depending on its role in the relative clause.
- In your sentence:
  - *päätös* is the object of *kadun* (*I regret*).
  - *katua* requires **partitive**.
  - So → **jota**.
